# TEDx talk — draft

> **Status:** 🟡 Draft outline | **Purpose:** Working draft for a TEDx speaker application and talk — the idea, the outline, and every data point behind it, sourced. | **Last Updated:** 2026-07-20

Built from the "spark" thesis already in `brand-narrative.md`, reshaped for TEDx's actual rules: an idea worth spreading, not a product pitch. Ordo7 gets one brief mention at the very end, if at all — the talk is the labor-shortage thesis on its own merits, which is also genuinely a stronger talk than a startup pitch would be.

## The one-sentence idea

*We're asking Project Managers to secretly do two jobs, because the profession meant to catch the slack — project controls — doesn't have enough people, and nobody's saying so out loud.*

## Outline

1. **Open on a real moment** — the 4:45pm-before-a-status-meeting scene, or a specific real instant from the K-12 or utility work where controls work landed on a PM's desk that nobody hired them to do. Needs a true, specific memory, not a composite.
2. **Name the pattern, then back it with the number** — this isn't just one person's experience; PMI says 25 million project professionals short globally by 2030, $345.5B in GDP on the line.
3. **Show where it's coming from** — the boom (data center construction spending up 688% since 2018) colliding with a workforce that's shrinking and moving (the California outmigration parallel — physical/people-capacity not keeping pace, same shape as the labor-supply problem).
4. **The real cost, made human** — a specific, real consequence of nobody watching a schedule full-time. Not abstract — an actual thing that happened.
5. **The turn** — this isn't a training problem or a laziness problem. It's a structural mismatch between project volume and specialist supply, and it's being treated as an individual failure instead of a named, measurable trend.
6. **Close** — brief personal note on what you did about it, then the idea to leave the room with: the shortage is real, it's measurable, and it's being absorbed silently by people who didn't sign up for it.

## The data, sourced (pulled 20 Jul 2026 — re-verify before using in a live talk, these move fast)

### The capital-projects boom (data centers)
- U.S. data center construction spending: **$128.4B projected for full-year 2026**
- Up **437% since 2021**, up **688% since 2018**
- Year-to-date 2026 spending is **4x the entire 2025 record**
- Data centers now **2.3% of all U.S. construction spending**
- Sources: [ConstructConnect, Jul 2026 report](https://news.constructconnect.com/july-2026-data-center-report-year-to-date-spending-four-times-the-2025-record), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-01/us-construction-spending-on-data-centers-eclipses-50-billion)

### The craft/trade labor shortage
- Construction needs **349,000–499,000 net new workers in 2026** just to stay in equilibrium (range reflects two different projections — ABC vs. another industry estimate; report the range, not a single cherry-picked number)
- **82%** of contractors report difficulty filling hourly craft positions, **80%** for salaried roles — highest in three years
- Workforce shortage is now the **#1 cause of project delays** — 45% of contractors report at least one delayed project from it this year
- Sources: [AGC News](https://news.agc.org/workforce-development/workforce-shortages-delay-projects/), [Westside Construction Group](https://www.buildwcg.com/blog-posts/construction-workforce-shortage-349000-workers-2026)

### The project-controls/PM talent gap specifically (the number the talk's spine should be built on)
- PMI: **25 million new project professionals needed globally by 2030** (2.3M/year), rising to **30 million by 2035**
- Global demand for project talent could grow **64% from 2025 to 2035**
- The gap could cost up to **$345.5B in lost global GDP by 2030**
- PM ranked the **12th fastest-growing job role globally** (World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs 2025)
- Context: ~40 million project professionals in the global workforce today, so this is roughly a 60%+ shortfall relative to current supply
- Sources: [PMI press release](https://www.pmi.org/about/press-media/2025/shortage-of-project-talent-endangers-global-growth), [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/25-million-new-project-professionals-are-needed-by-2030/), [PMI Global Project Management Talent Gap](https://www.pmi.org/learning/thought-leadership/global-project-management-talent-gap)

### The California migration parallel
- California lost **229,000+ residents net in 2025** (~230K/year ongoing pattern)
- Texas is the #1 destination for departing Californians (14.1% of outbound interest, 77,161 people per 2024 Census data)
- Arizona also a top-5 destination (52,383)
- Sources: [Stanford SIEPR](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/policy-brief/californias-population-drain), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/map-shows-states-causing-california-exodus-11399718)

## Applying to speak

TEDx (not TED itself, which is invite-only) is the accessible path — independently organized, TED-licensed local events with their own open speaker applications. Hard rule: **no product pitch, no self-promotion** — the talk has to stand as an idea on its own. Talks capped at 18 minutes; TEDx never pays speakers. Events plan 6+ months out, so apply early. No central application list — check event websites directly, or track open calls via the Speaker Accelerator newsletter (speakeraccelerator.substack.com).
