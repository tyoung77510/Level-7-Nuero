## Day 003 — Logic (DCMA 1/14)

An activity with no predecessor isn't a schedule — it's a guess.

Logic is the first of the DCMA's 14 schedule-health checks, and it's the simplest to understand: every activity needs something driving it (a predecessor) and something depending on it (a successor). Skip that, and a delay to that activity never ripples through to your finish date. The schedule looks fine. It isn't.

I'm running through all 14 DCMA checks here over the next few months — plain language, no scheduling certification required. Ordo7 flags every one of these automatically the moment you upload a schedule.

## Day 004 — Utility industry / safety

In utility work, a missed schedule isn't just late — it's a safety problem.

I spent time on a water utility maintenance program early in my career. Compliance-driven maintenance work doesn't get schedule extensions the way construction sometimes does — the regulatory clock doesn't care how busy your team is. Enforcing schedule discipline there cut safety incidents by 9%, because a rushed, out-of-sequence maintenance job is a safety risk before it's ever a schedule risk.

That's part of why I'm building Ordo7 to work across industries, not just construction. The DCMA checks are the same math everywhere. The stakes aren't.
